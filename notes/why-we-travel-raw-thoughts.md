# Why We Travel — Raw Thoughts

Captured on 11 November 2026 during the homepage review.

These are Vipin's unedited reflections. They are preserved as source material for future About Us copy and should not be treated as final website prose.

## How our travel philosophy evolved

I think our travel philosophy has evolved as we have traveled over the years. Initially it was just me and my wife and I think our focus was mainly seeing new sights, experiencing new cultures, trying out new foods.

As we kept traveling our interest grew. Five years into our travels we had our first kid and taking that first trip with a 6-month-old was quite a learning experience. We weren't really sure how our travel would change. Our travel did change. It slowed us down. We became more deliberate in how we spent the time and obviously we also started spending more time at a place just because we inherently took longer to visit places and experience culture.

The way we travel changed a lot but I think the way we take it is that it's part of growing up. Things you enjoy doing while traveling as a 20-year-old are fundamentally different from things you enjoy doing as a 40-something-year-old with two kids.

Having the first kid and traveling with a 6-month-old locally in Tasmania and then a 7-month-old to Japan was transformative. It set us up well because it gave us the confidence that we could still continue traveling even with kids. That's something I think we've seen a lot of families question. They feel that those initial years, when the kids are small, are very hard to travel in and they just stay put.

We took that as a challenge. I feel like both when we had the first kid and when we had the second kid, we actually had two long trips: a 3-week-long trip to Turkey and a month-long trip to Mexico and the U.S. That definitely reinforced our belief that traveling with young kids is totally possible.

Obviously you can't do the same things you did as just a couple in your 20s but you can still get a lot of value from enjoying a totally new culture, a totally new cuisine, and exploring parts of the world which you had only seen on TV or read in books.

I think it's a long-winded way to say the reason why we travel is really to challenge our assumptions and broaden our horizons, right? Growing up you always have a certain view of the world which you inherit from your parents, from your friends, from those who are around you. It's only when you travel outside of that bubble that you realize that not everything you've heard and believed growing up as a kid is true. Every place you travel to pushes the boundary that much more, where you learn something new and it kind of challenges the original assumptions you had.

That's one main reason. The other main reason is the food. We strongly believe that a way to experience a culture is via the food and its cuisine and both of us have an inherent passion for trying new foods. That just naturally translates very well with travel. The reason we travel is just to explore different cuisines. Although a place like Australia has so many cuisines because of the multicultural nature of the country, when you travel to the source, to the country, the food there and the ingredients there are just so completely different than what you get abroad.

That's the second reason. The third reason, I think, what I'm more thinking of of late is that as we have kids we want to instill that thought in them. Living in Sydney, living in Australia, you again come with a certain way of living life that you see around you. We want to give an opportunity for them to see a different part of the world, see how people live in that part of the world, and get an understanding that there's no right or wrong way to live life.

It's just because you've seen people around you do live life in a certain fashion, it doesn't mean that's the only way or that's the right way. That's the philosophy we are hoping that we will instill in our kids as they explore the world together with us.

Clearly they won't remember a whole lot of this when they grow up but I'm hoping it still sows some seeds in their minds, which will help broaden their horizons as they grow up.

## Early curiosity and the feeling of a new place

Growing up I've always been fascinated by other cultures. Every day when I used to read the newspaper, the Around the World section was the first thing I read because I was just fascinated by all the different stories I could read there. Growing up in India, exposure to the outside world (and this was before the internet) was very limited. You only had these little windows to understand what's happening outside India and this was my little window to the world.

When I only got the opportunity to travel abroad for the first time in my twenties (and same with my wife), it was just such an incredible feeling. For the first time you could experience something for the first time and that joy cannot be replicated in anything else. I remember reading a quote: "When was the last time you did something for the first time?" I get that feeling whenever I am in a new country in a totally unknown city, the first time I step out of the airport. That feeling is amazing. It's a little nerve-wracking but it is an amazing feeling to be out in the unknown when you don't know the language and you just try to figure out what to do.

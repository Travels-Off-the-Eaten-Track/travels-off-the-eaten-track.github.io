# Legacy Travel Notes — Research Index

Captured on 12 November 2026 from the five iCloud Notes folders named `Imported Notes 1` through `Imported Notes 5`.

This is a read-only, sanitised research record for the travel archive. Booking references, ticket numbers, passport details, loyalty numbers, phone numbers, private addresses and access instructions have deliberately been excluded.

## What the source contains

The imported folders are not a clean chronological archive. They contain:

- detailed day-by-day itineraries;
- restaurant and food research;
- booking confirmations;
- photo-heavy meal diaries with short captions and destination tags;
- duplicate notes across folders;
- newer Australian-era travel notes mixed with the older European archive;

This first pass prioritises journeys, places, food memories and narrative evidence. Dates below are based on the itinerary itself where available; a note's modification date is not treated as the travel date.

## Editorial patterns emerging from the archive

1. **Food is the organising memory.** Restaurant names, dishes and short judgments are often more precise than sightseeing notes.
2. **Trips combine one major landscape with several food anchors.** Examples include Iceland’s ring road, the Dolomites hikes, Sardinia’s coast and the Portuguese road trip.
3. **Birthdays and anniversaries create natural story hooks.** Burano and Tuscany are especially clear examples.
4. **The couple-era travel style is fast and ambitious.** Early journeys move frequently, use rental cars and fit multiple regions into a few days.
5. **The archive distinguishes research from lived experience.** “Best ever”, “excellent”, `best in town` and `once in a lifetime` are stronger signals than copied recommendation lists.
